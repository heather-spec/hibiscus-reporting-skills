# Gold-Standard Section — The Paragraph-Ordered Standard (v4.0)

This is the concrete model for every care-area section. The monitoring team adopted a **paragraph-ordered** structure: the section walks the measure's Consent Decree paragraphs in number order, gives each one a labeled block, synthesizes that paragraph's evidence into a tight account, then closes with the Compliance Determination, the three-row table, and the NOTES-TO-THE-MONITOR block.

Study the exemplar below, match its **structure, density, and register**, and fill it with the facts from the care-area source file at hand. The clinical facts in the exemplar are illustration only — scaffolding, never content to import.

> **This supersedes the older continuous-prose exemplars.** Earlier versions of this file taught sections built as flowing prose with bespoke italic sub-topics (Staffing / Oversight / Medication Administration). That organization is retired. Use those only for the sentence-level voice moves distilled in the "Voice & Sentence-Craft Library" at the end — never for structure. The spine is now the CD paragraph.

---

## The structure, in one place

Every section has this skeleton, in this order:

1. **Working header** — `Section [Letter] — [Care Area] [DRAFT]` (the `[Letter]`/`[DRAFT]` markers are stripped at assembly).
2. **Coverage line** — one sentence naming which paragraphs have current evidence and which had none this period (and therefore carry forward).
3. **The body — one block per CD paragraph in the measure, ascending order.** Each block:
   `Paragraph [NN] — [Topic]. [Synthesized narrative.]`
   - **Topic** is the CD requirement restated in 2–5 words, drawn from the verbatim decree text — *what the paragraph is supposed to show*. It is never a finding and is always derivable, even with zero evidence.
   - **Narrative** synthesizes that paragraph's evidence into one focused account (occasionally two short paragraphs for the most-evidenced provisions). It is **not** a record-by-record walk. Length tracks the evidence for that paragraph: the most-evidenced block is the longest; a thin one is three or four sentences.
4. **No-evidence paragraphs still get a block — as a placeholder:**
   `Paragraph [NN] — [Topic]. NO EVIDENCE.`
   Every paragraph in the measure appears, real or placeholder, so the section is a complete in-order audit of the whole measure. A NO EVIDENCE paragraph is not graded from this period; it carries its prior grade forward (flagged in NOTES) and is left out of the current table body.
5. **Compliance Determination paragraph** — `Compliance Determination:` (bold run) + the measure's exact boilerplate sentence + verdict verb.
6. **Three-row status table** — Non-Compliant / Partially Compliant / Substantially Compliant → plain paragraph numbers (sub-letters/romans preserved).
7. **`— NOTES TO THE MONITOR (delete before filing) —`** — the rich, sectioned block (see Exemplar 2).

The body runs in **paragraph order**; the table re-sorts the same graded paragraphs **by status**. Every graded block above appears in exactly one table row.

## Exemplar 1 — The section prose (paragraph-ordered body + determination + table)

**What this teaches:** the paragraph-block spine; topic titles drawn from the decree; synthesis instead of enumeration; the NO EVIDENCE placeholder; a two-handed block that carries both concern and credit; inline baseline only where a real then-vs-now exists; flat declarative voice throughout.

> Section A — General Medical & Nursing Care [DRAFT]
>
> CD paragraphs covered by current evidence: 30, 33, 34, 35, 36, 37, 38. Paragraphs 31 and 32 had no new evidence this period; each appears below as a placeholder block and its prior status carries forward pending the monitors' review.
>
> **Paragraph 30 — Appropriate clinical care.** The monitoring team's review of clinical care delivery identified gaps spanning assessment completion, timeliness of direct care, and the physical environment. Across six residents reviewed, monitors found required assessments missing in five — among them a complete absence of Braden Scale (the standard pressure-injury risk screen) documentation for one resident across a six-month period, a missing post-fall assessment, and weekly skin-assessment gaps of up to five weeks. During a February 2026 onsite visit, monitors observed a 21-minute delay in certified nursing assistant (CNA) response to a resident calling for help. A separate resident reported being unable to access his own room because accumulated belongings blocked wheelchair and Hoyer lift maneuverability — a situation a CNA confirmed and the administrator acknowledged required investigation.
>
> **Paragraph 31 — Care by contractors and outside providers.** NO EVIDENCE.
>
> **Paragraph 32 — Specialized clinical services.** NO EVIDENCE.
>
> **Paragraph 33 — Assessment, documentation, and clinical scrutiny.** The largest body of evidence this period concerns documentation that the monitoring team found does not withstand clinical scrutiny. The monitoring team reviewed records where physician orders specifically directed EMR documentation of skin checks, yet assessments appeared only on paper Treatment Administration Records (TARs) or not at all. Vital signs ordered monthly with direction to document in the Electronic Medical Record (EMR) were found on paper medication administration records for three consecutive months but were absent from the EMR, making clinical trending impractical. For one resident, TAR signatures for skin checks showed completion across 26 ordered dates while only approximately 54% had corresponding EMR entries with clinical findings; similar gaps recurred in other records, with six of twelve expected entries missing in one case and eleven missing entries across three months in another. Pain was documented as zero across 57 and 59 assessment opportunities for two residents who concurrently received pain medication, PRN Tylenol, and physician-documented pain up to 10/10. Wound entries frequently consisted of initials without measurements, characteristics, or status. Monitors also identified direct internal contradictions, including an investigative report attributing a wound discovery to rounds on a date when no corresponding finding appears in that day's note, and a medically complex resident with critical BNP values — a lab marker of cardiac strain — of 312 and 363 whose nursing notes were described as containing nothing substantive. The Medical Director acknowledged the record is not well organized. By April 2026, monitors observed improved collaborative problem-solving, with facility staff proposing specific actionable steps including standardizing skin check scheduling on TARs and engaging technical resources to explore electronic system solutions.
>
> **Paragraph 34 — Uniform policies and procedures.** The facility reported substantial progress in policy standardization across the three homes, with approximately 87 policies completed in the first year and work continuing. Three wound-care policies were updated in October 2025, and staff confirmed that policies are being aligned across all three facilities. The monitoring team also noted the facility's initiative in developing a standardized process for the physician to indicate which consultant recommendations he endorses. However, existing policies were not consistently implemented: despite a policy requiring Braden Scale assessment on admission and weekly for four subsequent weeks, one resident was missing four weekly assessments after readmission, which the monitoring team characterized as a system that is not working. Staff estimated only 60–80% of policy revisions are captured in Quality Assurance meeting minutes, and no formal policy governing the review cycle itself could be confirmed.
>
> **Paragraph 35 — Policy review conforming to Consent Decree requirements.** The policy revision and review efforts described under paragraph 34 are ongoing, and monitoring team oversight of this process continues. However, the absence of a complete audit trail for policy revisions — with staff acknowledging that a portion of changes are not captured in Quality Assurance minutes — limits the Agreement Coordinator's ability to fully verify that all revised policies conform to Consent Decree requirements.
>
> **Paragraph 36 — Facility Assessment.** The Facility Assessment had not been updated to reflect census growth from approximately 150 to 204 residents or the restructuring of the restorative program. Staff reported the assessment was due in August 2026. Paragraph 36 requires the assessment to be updated whenever there is any change to services that would require substantial modification; the growth in census and program restructuring constitute such a change.
>
> **Paragraph 37 — Electronic medical records.** The Baseline Report identified that [Facility] had not implemented and maintained electronic resident medical and clinical care records in an organized, readily accessible manner. That finding remains evident. Clinical work logged on paper TARs was repeatedly not entered into the EMR despite physician orders, and on one visit monitors could not locate skin assessments in the EMR until staff produced paper copies. Laboratory results were described by the Medical Director as delivered unsorted and mixed among different residents. Multiple versions of assessment forms coexist — two Braden Scale versions and four weekly skin-assessment versions — compounding inconsistency. The monitoring team described the documentation systems as not reconciled and noted this remains a point of disagreement with DMAVA leadership. The Medical Director expressed support for the planned Unity EHR implementation, which will replace the current system and integrate physician orders, medication administration records, TARs, and clinical documentation. The monitoring team acknowledges that no short-term resolution exists for the current EMR situation and will continue to monitor progress toward implementation.
>
> **Paragraph 38 — Staff qualifications to conduct assessments.** During one onsite visit, a charge nurse was uncertain of her role in care-plan meetings and did not reference Situation, Background, Assessment, and Recommendation (SBAR) documentation or Braden screening in her workflow, indicating a role-clarity gap for assessment responsibilities on at least one unit. This single observation provides limited evidence regarding staff qualifications more broadly. The monitoring team believes the Resident Assessment Coordinators at [Facility] have the necessary skills and qualifications to accurately complete the Minimum Data Set (MDS), the federally mandated comprehensive resident assessment used to develop and update individualized care plans.
>

The table below details the compliance status for each provision within this Remedial Measure.

### Craft moves

1. **Coverage line sets the scope honestly.** It names which paragraphs carry current evidence and tells the reader the empty ones (31, 32) carry forward — before the body. The reader knows the whole measure was considered.
2. **Every block opens with `Paragraph NN — Topic.`** The topic is the decree requirement in a few words (30 = "appropriate clinical care," 37 = "electronic … records," 33 = "documented … to withstand clinical scrutiny"). It maps one-to-one to the CD text and never asserts a finding.
3. **Synthesis, not enumeration.** ¶33 carries the most evidence and self-labels it ("The largest body of evidence this period concerns…"), then folds many record reviews into one woven account with exact figures (26 ordered dates / ~54%; six of twelve; 57 and 59 pain opportunities; BNP 312 and 363). The individual Record #s are **not** in the prose — they live in the crosswalk in NOTES. This is the discipline that keeps detail from overwhelming the section.
4. **Density is per-block.** ¶35 and ¶36 are three to four sentences because that is all their evidence supports; ¶33 runs long because it must. Length is an output of the evidence, set block by block — never an imposed target.
5. **NO EVIDENCE placeholder.** ¶31 and ¶32 each get a block with the topic and `NO EVIDENCE.` — nothing more. They are absent from the table (not graded this period); their carry-forward is handled in NOTES. When a paragraph *does* have evidence, the block is written out instead.
6. **Two-handed blocks.** ¶34 leads with credit ("approximately 87 policies completed") then pivots with "However," to the implementation gap. ¶33 closes on the April improvement. Concern and credit live inside the same block, sequenced as the source presents them — no manufactured balance.
7. **Inline baseline only where earned.** Only ¶37 invokes the Baseline Report, because only there is a genuine then-vs-now ("identified that [Facility] had not implemented … That finding remains evident"). The other blocks never mention baseline.
8. **Flat statement of the requirement, then stop.** ¶36: "Paragraph 36 requires the assessment to be updated whenever there is any change … the growth in census and program restructuring constitute such a change." It states the requirement and the observed fact; it does not editorialize about consequences.
9. **Defined terms on first use, throughout** — Braden Scale, CNA, TAR, EMR, SBAR, MDS — each parenthetically expanded the first time, for the court reader.
10. **The table mirrors the body, re-sorted.** The graded paragraphs (30, 33, 34, 35, 36, 37, 38) each land in exactly one row; the worst rows (33, 37 Non-Compliant) drive the "has not reached" verdict. 31 and 32 do not appear — NO EVIDENCE, carried forward in NOTES.

## Exemplar 2 — The NOTES-to-the-monitor block (the model)

**What this teaches:** the sectioned NOTES format the team adopted. This is the verification and reconciliation layer — everything a monitor needs to lock the section. It is drawn from a parallel Section A draft (a different facility), shown here to model the **format and the crosswalk's granularity**; in a real section every prose finding traces to a crosswalk entry below, and every grade in the table is one of these proposals.

The block is fenced, placed after the table, and organized into these named subsections **in this order**:

```
— NOTES TO THE MONITOR (delete before filing) —

PURPOSE OF THIS SECTION: [care area], CD ¶¶ [range] (internal tag prefix [letter]), [Facility]. Drafted solely from [source-file path] ([N] observations, dated [first] through [last]). No facts imported from the filed reports or any other source. "[Facility]" substituted at assembly.

DETERMINATION RATIONALE (all grades are PROPOSALS — confirm/adjust each):
- Overall verdict driver: [which paragraphs drive the headline, and why].
- ¶[NN] ([short requirement]) — [PROPOSED GRADE]. [What drove it. Where a harder grade is arguable, "CONSIDER [grade]:" with the specific findings that might support it — flagged, not asserted.]
- (one line per graded paragraph)

PRIOR-PERIOD GRADE CONTINUITY — REQUIRED: Pull the prior report's paragraph-level grades for ¶¶[range]; confirm trajectory (improving / holding / declining) for each; adjust any proposal that conflicts with the prior anchor. Do NOT manufacture a Baseline comparison the source does not document.

MISSING / EXTERNAL DATA TO SUPPLY (not in the observations — do not invent):
- [each external figure or document the prose needs: CMS QM %, PBJ tables, audit results, records requested-but-not-produced, etc.]

SCOPE / OVERLAP NOTES:
- [findings deliberately NOT drafted here because they belong to another care-area section; double-counting to watch at assembly]

ANONYMIZATION CONFIRMED (no names above the fence):
- Staff converted to roles: [name → role, …]. Monitoring-team members appear nowhere above the fence.
- [any data-quality flags from the source — e.g., mislabeled placeholders]. No resident names; no case/docket number; no observation IDs above the fence.

SOURCE-OBSERVATION CROSSWALK (every substantive finding above traces to ≥1 of these):
- Rec#[ ] | obs [id] | [date] | [type] | [one-line of what it contributed] → ¶¶[ ].
- (one line per observation drawn upon)

RECONCILIATION QUESTIONS FOR THE MONITOR:
1. [lock the proposed grades; decide any flagged Partial-vs-Non-Compliant or defer-vs-grade question]
2. [confirm prior-period grades and trajectory]
3. [supply the external/audit data flagged above]
4. [any section-specific confirmations]
```

### Why the NOTES block looks like this

- **Every grade is labeled a PROPOSAL, with a "CONSIDER" alternative where a harder grade is arguable.** Determinations are the monitors' call; the draft proposes and flags, never asserts a grade it privately doubts.
- **The crosswalk is the spine of trust.** Every substantive prose finding traces to at least one Record#/obs line. If a finding cannot be tied to an observation here, it does not belong in the prose. Record#/obs IDs appear ONLY here, never above the fence.
- **External data is named, never invented.** CMS QM %, PBJ tables, audit results, unproduced records — each is a specific request to the monitor, with a marked spot, not a number in the prose.
- **Scope/overlap protects against double-counting** when the full report is assembled from thirteen care areas drawn from overlapping observations.
- **Anonymization is affirmatively confirmed**, including any source data-quality flags, so the monitor can trust the prose is names-free.

## Voice & Sentence-Craft Library

These are the transferable sentence-level moves from the team's filed reports. They apply *inside* each paragraph block — they do **not** change the paragraph-ordered structure.

- **Credit-first framing within a deficiency block.** Open on what is standing and working, then pivot to the gap with a continuity verb. "The facility reported substantial progress … However, existing policies were not consistently implemented." Leading with the functioning system earns the criticism its credibility.
- **Continuity verbs signal a tracked, longitudinal picture.** "continues to," "remains," "has not yet" — the same verb can carry credit ("continues to educate") or deficiency ("continues to struggle"), marking a known, monitored issue rather than a fresh discovery.
- **Quantify the credit and the concern alike.** Exact X-of-N beats adjectives: "required assessments missing in five" of six; "20 reviews for residents under three other attending physicians." A counted figure attached to a dated event converts a soft claim into something auditable.
- **Epistemic honesty.** State the limit of what the record proves: "The monitoring team could not determine whether treatments were completed but not documented, or not completed at all." This restraint is what makes the unqualified findings elsewhere trustworthy.
- **Commendation anchored to conduct, not adjectives.** Praise leads with verifiable status ("licensed, credentialed, and well-qualified") and defensible descriptors ("consistent, competent, and appropriate"); the team's own reaction ("remains impressed") is immediately leashed to an observed behavior. Never free-floating ("robust," "seamless").
- **Data paired with its comparator.** When the source supplies a figure, embed it with the benchmark and period the source itself states ("compared to the national average of 3.9 hours"). Never a number without its comparator; never a comparator the source does not give.
- **The concessive pivot, sourced both ways.** "However," / "Despite," / "While" pivots from credit to residual concern only when both halves are in the source — not to manufacture balance.
- **Plain statement of the standard, never a consequence.** "Standard of practice requires documenting medications at the time of administration." State the requirement and what was observed; do not argue toward downstream harm ("raises questions about whether…," "increases the potential for preventable harm…" are banned).

## How to use this

1. Open the care-area source file. Identify which CD paragraphs of the measure it carries evidence for.
2. Draft the body **paragraph by paragraph in ascending order**, one block each. Every paragraph in the measure gets a block — synthesized narrative where there is evidence, `Topic. NO EVIDENCE.` where there is not.
3. Keep each block's density proportional to its evidence. Synthesize; push Record#-level detail to the crosswalk.
4. Use the Voice & Craft Library inside the blocks; weave baseline only where a real then-vs-now exists.
5. Close with the Compliance Determination (the measure's exact boilerplate), the three-row table (graded paragraphs only; NO EVIDENCE paragraphs carried forward in NOTES), and the full sectioned NOTES block.
6. Fill the blocks with the **source file's** facts — the exemplar's numbers and names are scaffolding, never imported. Use `[Facility]`, no case number, no resident or staff names, no observation IDs in the prose.
