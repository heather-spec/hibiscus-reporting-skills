# Gold-Standard Sections

A concrete reference for the section-writer skill. Each care-area section in a Consent Decree monitoring report is built from the same parts and the same voice. This document shows three fully worked examples spanning the report's range — a data-dense substantially-compliant section, a positive "reached compliance" section, and a deficiency-with-progress section — so the writer can match an exemplar's shape, register, and structure, then fill it with the facts from the source file at hand.

---

## Cross-Cutting Patterns

Every good section, regardless of outcome, shares these conventions. Treat them as defaults; the exemplars below show them in action.

**Flat declarative voice.** State findings as conclusions, not arguments. The monitor reports what it observed and what the record shows; it does not advocate. When the team voices its own reaction ("the monitoring team continues to be impressed"), it is immediately leashed to an observable behavior. Praise is anchored to verifiable status (licensed, credentialed) and defensible descriptors (consistent, competent, appropriate), never to free-floating adjectives.

**Evidence narrated with specifics and counts.** Replace soft claims with falsifiable detail: a counted figure ("20 reviews"), a dated event ("the July quarterly QAPI meeting," "In June 2025"), an itemized agenda, a named multidisciplinary roster. Sourcing is explicit — "he reported," "the Medical Director stated" — so the reader can tell what the monitor independently confirmed from what a staff member asserted.

**Inline, sparing baseline.** When a metric has improved, weave the prior figure into the same sentence as the current one and label the trajectory ("an improvement from the first quarter's 76% compliance"). No separate "baseline" block, no before/after table. The comparison should be unavoidable and stated as the writer's own conclusion.

**Density tracks evidence.** Most of the section is prose. Break into a list or nested list only where the data is genuinely tabular — a parent total decomposed across named sub-categories (shifts, units). Present shift-by-shift compliance figures inline as a series when they tell a diagnostic story in sentence form. The discipline: bullets earn their place; if prose would bury a pattern, list it; otherwise write the sentence.

**Acknowledge the downside, then interpret.** Never let numbers sit inert and never whitewash. Name the genuine risk (agency-staff continuity, medication-error potential), then reframe the same data as evidence of a working practice. State the limits of what the record can prove ("the monitoring team could not determine whether treatments were completed but not documented, or not completed at all"). This epistemic honesty is what makes the unqualified claims elsewhere trustworthy.

**The Compliance Determination paragraph plus three-row table.** Every section closes with a fixed form:

> Compliance Determination: Based on the above observations [Facility] has reached substantial compliance with the [Care Area] requirements outlined in the Consent Decree. The table below details the compliance status for each provision within this Remedial Measure.

The label is bold. "Based on the above observations" ties the verdict to the evidence just presented. The operative phrase is either "has reached substantial compliance" or "has not reached substantial compliance" — identical across sections, only the polarity changes. Then a sentence hands off to the table. The table always lists all three status tiers (Non-Compliant, Partially Compliant, Substantially Compliant) even when rows are empty — the empty rows are load-bearing, affirmatively showing nothing fell into those categories. Each populated row maps the status to exact Consent Decree paragraph numbers, making the finding auditable. A single Partially Compliant provision can hold an entire Remedial Measure short of substantial compliance while the table preserves credit for everything that works.

**Anonymization, no observation IDs in prose.** Use `[Facility]` for any facility name. No case or docket number. No resident or staff names — refer to roles (the Medical Director, the DON, a CNA, an agency nurse). Do not embed observation or finding ID numbers in the narrative; the Consent Decree paragraph numbers belong only in the determination table.

## Exemplar 1 — Data-Dense / Substantially Compliant: Staffing

**What this teaches:** how to present quantitative CMS payroll-based journal (PBJ) data — every figure paired with its national benchmark, facility hours in hours-and-minutes against decimal national averages — and how to segment a long section with italic run-in sub-headings without promoting them to full headers.

### The gold-standard section

> Staffing
>
> [Facility] continues to maintain sufficient clinical staff to meet resident acuity needs. Clinical staffing aligns with New Jersey State Guidelines for nursing assistants and often exceeds those ratios based on residents' acuity levels. Clinical staff demonstrated competence when providing direct care to residents.
>
> For example, the monitor observed a CNA perform a competent bed bath and transfer for a resident. The CNA was gentle, respectful, and ensured privacy, allowing the resident to choose clothing. Despite the resident being uncooperative with foot and genital/buttocks care, the CNA showed patience and explained the need for thorough cleansing to maintain skin integrity.
>
> The monitoring team examined a QAPI project, led by nursing leadership, that focuses on consistent adherence to staffing standards. This study's data collection involves auditing all shifts to verify compliance with New Jersey's minimum staffing requirements based on residents' daily acuity levels and to track instances where Restorative CNAs (RNAs) are diverted from their restorative roles to general CNA duties.
>
> The June QAPI report confirmed that New Jersey's minimum staffing requirements, based on resident acuity, were met or exceeded during April, May, and June. The report also indicated the reassignment of Registered Nurse Assistants (RNAs) to Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA) duties for a total of six days: three days in April, two days in May, and one day in June. Information specific to June staffing detailed the following use of agency staff and home health aides:
>
> - Agency Staff: 72 nursing shifts were covered by agency staff
>   - 7am - 3pm shift: 26
>   - 3pm - 11pm shift: 25
>   - 11pm - 7am shift: 24
> - Home Health Aides: 53 shifts utilized Home Health Aides as 1:1 companions
>   - 7am - 3pm shift: 36
>   - 3pm - 11pm shift: 9
>   - 11pm - 7am shift: 8
>
> The utilization of agency nurses in long-term care introduces potential risks to the continuity and consistency of care, primarily due to their limited familiarity with residents and facility protocols. This may increase the potential for adverse events, such as medication errors. This specificity of agency staff and home health aid data reported above demonstrates the facility's use of data to help determine if there is a particular shift on which recruitment efforts should be focused.
>
> In response to the need to use RNAs as CNAs, four full-time RNAs were hired for the Restorative Program, which transitioned from RNAs reporting to the Rehab Department (a contracted service) to the Nursing Department. The previous five RNAs were transitioned back to their assigned units on July 14, 2025. (The monitoring team does not know how many hours per week those five RNAs worked.)
>
> *CMS Staffing Performance*
>
> [Facility] has a 5-Star staffing rating on CMS Care Compare. The following payroll-based journal (PBJ) staffing data reflects information about direct clinical care staff reported through the second calendar quarter of 2025:
>
> - Total nurse staff hours per resident per day: 5 hours and 43 minutes compared to the national average of 3.9 hours
> - Weekend nurse staffing: 5 hours and 18 minutes compared to the national average of 3.42 hours
> - RN hours per resident per day: 1 hour and 1 minute compared to the national average of 0.68 hours
> - LPN/LVN hours per resident per day: 1 hour and 10 minutes compared to the national average of 0.87 hours
> - Nursing assistant hours per resident per day: 3 hours and 22 minutes compared to the national average of 2.34 hours
> - Total nursing staff turnover: 21.5% compared to a national average of 46.4%
> - RN staff turnover: 38.9% compared to the national average of 43.6%
>
> *Leadership Transitions*
>
> The [Facility] DON recently resigned, and a new staff member recently hired in the ADON position is serving as interim DON. This appointment represents a positive development for the facility, as the individual is highly qualified for the role and brings extensive nursing experience, including prior tenure as a DON. The monitoring team observed her excellent leadership skills, and her valuable background in education positions her as a strong resource for mentorship within the nursing staff. Accordingly, the monitoring team has no reservations regarding her ability to fill the role.
>
> Compliance Determination: Based on the above observations [Facility] has reached substantial compliance with the Staffing requirements outlined in the Consent Decree. The table below details the compliance status for each provision within this Remedial Measure.
>
> | Compliance Status | Corresponding Consent Decree Paragraphs |
> | --- | --- |
> | Non-Compliant | |
> | Partially Compliant | |
> | Substantially Compliant | 81, 82(a-d) |

### Craft moves

1. **Section opener — capability assertion with the governing standard named.** "[Facility] continues to maintain sufficient clinical staff to meet resident acuity needs. Clinical staffing aligns with New Jersey State Guidelines for nursing assistants and often exceeds those ratios based on residents' acuity levels." Opens with the bottom-line conclusion and immediately anchors it to the external authority the facility is measured against. "Continues" signals a recurring monitoring report tracking sustained compliance. Every number that follows reads as evidence for an already-stated conclusion.

2. **Concrete observed-care vignette to ground the abstract metric.** "the monitor observed a CNA perform a competent bed bath and transfer... Despite the resident being uncooperative with foot and genital/buttocks care, the CNA showed patience and explained the need for thorough cleansing to maintain skin integrity." One human, sensory anecdote demonstrates that competence is quality of direct care, not just adequate ratios. The uncooperative-resident detail shows the monitor witnessed real conditions, not a staged demonstration.

3. **Data-source provenance before data — naming the QAPI project that generated the numbers.** "The monitoring team examined a QAPI project... This study's data collection involves auditing all shifts to verify compliance with New Jersey's minimum staffing requirements... and to track instances where Restorative CNAs (RNAs) are diverted from their restorative roles." Establishes chain of custody: the facility self-audits all shifts and the monitor reviewed that audit. Defining the RNA/CNA-diversion problem here makes the later six-days figure and the four-RNA hire read as cause-and-effect.

4. **Sparing, purposeful use of a nested bulleted data list.** The agency-staff and home-health-aide totals (72, 53) decomposed across three named shifts. Nesting by shift makes the distribution scannable (home-health aides cluster on days, 36 of 53). The bullets earn their place because prose would bury the per-shift pattern.

5. **Interpreting the data immediately after presenting it — risk note plus the why-it-matters.** "The utilization of agency nurses... introduces potential risks to the continuity and consistency of care... This specificity of agency staff and home health aid data... demonstrates the facility's use of data to help determine if there is a particular shift on which recruitment efforts should be focused." Names the genuine downside so the report doesn't read as a whitewash, then reframes the same data as a strength. The balanced read is what makes a favorable determination defensible.

6. **Closing a loop with a dated corrective action and a candid knowledge gap.** "four full-time RNAs were hired for the Restorative Program... The previous five RNAs were transitioned back to their assigned units on July 14, 2025. (The monitoring team does not know how many hours per week those five RNAs worked.)" Resolves the diversion problem with a structural fix and an exact date. The parenthetical admission of a data gap is a signature credibility move.

7. **Italic inline sub-heading to segment a long section without a full header.** *CMS Staffing Performance* — an italicized run-in heading, distinct from the bold care-area header, introduces the PBJ block. This lightweight device organizes the section into legible sub-topics (observed care → QAPI audit → agency data → CMS metrics → leadership) without fragmenting it.

8. **The PBJ data convention — every metric paired with its national average.** Lead with the 5-Star rating and the reporting period (Q2 2025) to frame the source, then give a parallel-structured bullet per measure, always "compared to the national average of X." Facility hours stated in hours-and-minutes ("5 hours and 43 minutes"), national averages in CMS's native decimal hours ("3.9") — each source expressed in its own native form. Pairing every figure with a benchmark lets the numbers argue the case (turnover 21.5% vs 46.4%; RN turnover the one metric still near the national line at 38.9% vs 43.6%).

9. **Folding a leadership change into a forward-looking confidence statement, not a risk flag.** The DON resignation is reported plainly, then the interim's qualifications are built into an evidence-based case (prior DON tenure, observed leadership, education background for mentorship), landing on explicit reassurance — "no reservations." This keeps a personnel disruption from destabilizing the determination.

10. **The "has reached substantial compliance" determination paragraph.** Bold label, "Based on the above observations" clause tying verdict to evidence, the present-perfect operative phrase signaling an achieved status, the named Remedial Measure, and a hand-off to the table. The formula is identical across care areas, so the verdict is instantly locatable.

11. **Paired three-tier compliance table mapping status to paragraph numbers.** All three tiers listed even though two are empty — the empty rows affirmatively show nothing fell into those categories. The exact paragraph numbers (81, 82(a-d)) make the finding auditable and show every enumerated provision was evaluated.

## Exemplar 2 — Positive / Reached Compliance: Oversight and Management of Medical Care

**What this teaches:** how to credit a facility — including praising a named role like the Medical Director — without sliding into promotion: anchor commendation to counted, dated, itemized conduct; surface residual concerns and show their corrective arc; and reconcile a "substantial compliance" headline with one provision still only Partially Compliant.

### The gold-standard section

> Oversight and Management of Medical Care
>
> [Facility]'s licensed, credentialed, and well-qualified Medical Director continues to provide consistent, competent, and appropriate supervision of clinical services. He remains significantly involved in the facility's daily operations through his attendance and participation in morning clinical meetings, wrap-up sessions, and various QAPI meetings. The monitoring team continues to be impressed by his ability to initiate interdisciplinary discussions that frequently incorporate valuable teaching moments.
>
> Drawing on his experience with The Society for Post-Acute and Long-Term Care Medicine (formerly the American Medical Directors Association), the Medical Director has been conducting chart reviews. At the July quarterly QAPI meeting, he reported completing 20 reviews for residents under the care of three other attending physicians. He reaffirmed his dedication to clinical excellence by emphasizing SBARs, documentation, and communication, and by holding staff accountable in these specific areas.
>
> The Medical Director actively educates staff and defines clear performance expectations for all healthcare professionals, including other physicians. In June 2025, the Medical Director and other [Facility] leaders hosted a physician meeting addressing several key topics: Consent Decree updates, CMS regulatory requirements, hospitalizations and emergency department utilization, antibiotic stewardship, pharmacy recommendations, the Medical Director's role, and unnecessary medication use.
>
> During an interview with the monitoring team, the Medical Director reported that two providers are no longer employed at the facility. He expressed concern that they were not meeting appropriate performance standards. When he communicated these expectations, both providers chose to resign rather than commit to necessary performance improvement. The Medical Director and his colleagues have since assumed care of these residents.
>
> The monitoring team noted inadequate resident assessment documentation by a provider, including repeated vital signs for three months and a question mark notation for significant weight changes. The Medical Director stated he identified these concerns, addressed them with the provider, and resolved the situation.
>
> The Medical Director has not received an external review of his own direct care performance. DMAVA Leadership intends to have the Medical Directors at [Facility] and [Facility] conduct peer reviews for each other. This process will begin in early 2026 after the new Medical Director at [Facility] has been in place for several months.
>
> Compliance Determination: Based on the above observations the [Facility] facility has reached substantial compliance with the Oversight and Management of Medical Care requirements outlined in the Consent Decree. The table below details the compliance status for each provision within this Remedial Measure.
>
> | Compliance Status | Corresponding Consent Decree Paragraphs |
> | --- | --- |
> | Non-Compliant | |
> | Partially Compliant | 54e |
> | Substantially Compliant | 52, 53, 54a-d |

### Craft moves

1. **Opening commendation anchored to credentials, then conduct.** "[Facility]'s licensed, credentialed, and well-qualified Medical Director continues to provide consistent, competent, and appropriate supervision of clinical services." Praise leads with verifiable status and defensible descriptors rather than effusive adjectives. "Continues to" signals sustained performance across reporting periods — what substantial compliance rewards. Reads as an assessment, not a testimonial.

2. **Naming the monitor's own impression, but leashed to observable behavior.** "The monitoring team continues to be impressed by his ability to initiate interdisciplinary discussions that frequently incorporate valuable teaching moments." The rare subjective reaction ("impressed") stays credible because it is immediately grounded in concrete behavior, not floating flattery. This is the template for how the team is allowed to editorialize when crediting.

3. **Quantifying the credit.** "At the July quarterly QAPI meeting, he reported completing 20 reviews for residents under the care of three other attending physicians." A counted figure attached to a dated event converts a soft claim of diligence into something verifiable in the record. Note the careful sourcing ("he reported") rather than the team asserting it as independently confirmed.

4. **Itemized evidence of leadership.** Rather than "the Medical Director provides strong leadership," the section lists the actual June 2025 physician-meeting agenda (Consent Decree updates, CMS requirements, hospitalizations/ED utilization, antibiotic stewardship, pharmacy recommendations, the Medical Director's role, unnecessary medication use). The specificity does the persuading; a dated meeting with a serious topic list reads as documentation, not advocacy.

5. **Surfacing a hard fact and crediting the response, not glossing it.** "both providers chose to resign rather than commit to necessary performance improvement. The Medical Director and his colleagues have since assumed care of these residents." A credit-bearing section states the uncomfortable fact plainly, then shows the closed loop. This demonstrates accountability is working — precisely the argument for substantial compliance.

6. **The residual-concern arc: identify, address, resolve.** "The monitoring team noted inadequate resident assessment documentation by a provider, including repeated vital signs for three months and a question mark notation for significant weight changes. The Medical Director stated he identified these concerns, addressed them with the provider, and resolved the situation." The monitor reports the problem in its own voice with vivid specifics, then attributes the remedy to the Medical Director ("stated he") — crediting the corrective action without vouching for it. The three-verb cadence mirrors the compliance arc.

7. **Leaving one concern openly unresolved with a dated plan.** "The Medical Director has not received an external review of his own direct care performance... This process will begin in early 2026 after the new Medical Director at [Facility] has been in place for several months." Even when crediting, the team refuses to declare a gap closed before it is. Stated flatly ("has not received") and paired with a specific forward plan and timeframe. This candor about what is still pending keeps a positive section from becoming promotional.

8. **Determination headline that matches the body's balance.** "has reached substantial compliance" is the positive counterpart to the chapter's recurring "has not reached substantial compliance." "Based on the above observations" ties verdict to cited evidence; the formula is identical to negative determinations, only the polarity changes — which keeps the report's voice consistent regardless of outcome.

9. **Table reconciles a substantial-compliance verdict with a lingering partial.** One provision (54e) remains Partially Compliant even as the section earns the positive headline, proving "substantial compliance" is a holistic judgment, not a claim of perfection. The empty Non-Compliant row and the bulk of provisions in Substantially Compliant visually justify the determination, while 54e quietly preserves the residual concern (the pending external peer review).

## Exemplar 3 — Deficiency with Progress: Medication Administration

**What this teaches:** how to sustain a two-handed posture across a long section — crediting real, quantified improvement while still landing on "has not reached substantial compliance" — using continuity verbs, inline baseline-weaving, shift-by-shift data as prose, a full system-narrative arc (implementation → optimism → observed shortfall → root-cause diagnosis), and a determination table that parks only the failing provisions in Partially Compliant.

### The gold-standard section

> Medication Administration
>
> [Facility] continues to educate and competency test nursing staff on safe medication dispensation and administration. The leadership team has established a system to accurately track medication administration variances. Quarterly QAPI meetings include reports on medication variances with data on total medication errors, errors resulting in resident adverse outcomes, nurse type (registered nurse or licensed practical nurse), error type (e.g., transcription, wrong time, wrong dose), and the shift when the error occurred. During the second quarter of 2025, only five errors were reported with no adverse effects on residents. Staff received education, and the Consultant Pharmacist, Pharmacy, Physicians, and interdisciplinary team investigated all errors and provided recommendations for improvement.
>
> [Facility] nursing staff continues to struggle with consistently administering residents' medications and treatments within the correct timeframe, particularly during the 9:00 a.m. medication pass. Recognizing this issue, the clinical team, including the medical director, reviewed the most common medications to reduce the 9:00 a.m. medication administration burden. The team eliminated sliding scale insulin administration, adjusted vital sign parameters, and modified medication times when possible (e.g., changing from 9:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m.).
>
> Nursing leadership initiated a QAPI study during the first quarter of 2025 to improve timely medication administration performance. Data collected over the second quarter of 2025 showed 73% compliance on the 7-3 shift, 79% on the 3-11 shift, and 100% on the 11-7 shift. Overall quarterly compliance was 84%, an improvement from the first quarter's 76% compliance. A root cause analysis identified the most common reasons for late medication administration: starting the medication pass late, large quantities of medications (particularly on the day shift), and interruptions during medication administration, including answering phone calls, assisting with care delivery, answering questions, and performing necessary treatments. The team identified improvement actions including staff education, Medication Variance Committee involvement, and continued auditing until reaching at least 80% compliance. Staff education included reinservicing nursing staff on Medication Administration Policy & Procedure, providing 1:1 education to minimize or stop medication pass interruptions, and ensuring timely medication pass starts.
>
> In August 2025, [Facility] implemented the Passport Medication Administration System (Passport). The monitoring team interviewed several nurses who expressed confidence that the system will increase efficiency once nurses become more comfortable with it. However, during the October 2025 onsite visit, the monitoring team observed that on two units, three of four nurses did not complete the 9:00 a.m. medication pass on time. One nurse stated that when a resident's scheduled medication is not in the dispensed envelope, she must go to the Passport dispensing machine to retrieve it, causing delays in medication administration completion.
>
> The monitoring team's review of Treatment Administration Records (TARs) found that nurses do not consistently document treatment completion. One TAR lacked any initials indicating completion for treatments scheduled on a weekend 3-11 shift. The monitoring team discussed this with the DON, who determined an agency nurse was responsible for those treatments. The DON contacted the agency nurse, who reported completing all treatments despite the missing documentation. Another TAR lacked initials for all treatments scheduled on a weekday 7-3 shift. The monitoring team could not determine whether treatments were completed but not documented, or not completed at all. Per usual and customary practice, nurses should document treatments as they complete them.
>
> [Facility] nursing staff do not consistently ensure accurate, effective, and timely medication administration documentation. During two separate onsite visits, the monitoring team observed two nurses paging through medication administration binders and making entries on various pages after completing medication administration. Standard of practice requires documenting medications at the time of administration.
>
> Prior to the Consent Decree requirements, [Facility]'s Pharmacy and Therapeutics committee met quarterly to address medication variances. [Facility] recently increased meeting frequency to monthly to comply with Consent Decree requirements. The committee includes at least one QAPI committee member and a nurse involved in day-to-day medication administration. The monitoring team confirmed that all committee members have received QAPI training.
>
> The October 2025 Medication Variance Committee meeting included interdisciplinary discussion about Passport implementation. The Committee identified problems and received valuable input from nursing supervisors to help identify solutions. The discussion revealed that Passport capacity concerns are the biggest issue. Specifically, physicians order large quantities of medications with varying doses for each resident. Each dose requires its own Passport slot, limiting capacity for additional medications. The committee also identified that census growth may result in insufficient machine capacity. The committee will continue reviewing currently dispensed medications and doses to determine whether greater efficiencies can be achieved.
>
> [Facility] nursing staff record the effectiveness of as-needed medication on the reverse side of the medication administration record. Nurses' notes also show documentation of the effectiveness of ordered lotions and creams. The pharmacy consultant routinely reviews residents' medication regimens and makes recommendations regarding medication appropriateness and suggested modifications. Physicians review these documented consults in residents' medical records.
>
> Compliance Determination: Based on the above observations the [Facility] facility has not reached substantial compliance with the Medication Administration requirements outlined in the Consent Decree. The table below details the compliance status for each provision within this Remedial Measure.
>
> | Compliance Status | Corresponding Consent Decree Paragraphs |
> | --- | --- |
> | Non-Compliant | |
> | Partially Compliant | 50c, 50d |
> | Substantially Compliant | 50a, 50b, 50e, 50f, 50g, 50h |

### Craft moves

1. **Opening (credit-first framing).** "[Facility] continues to educate and competency test nursing staff... The leadership team has established a system to accurately track medication administration variances." The section opens on what is standing and working, not on the deficiency. "Continues to" presupposes prior credit and signals an ongoing practice. Leading with functioning systems earns the later criticism its credibility, and front-loads the substantially-compliant provisions the table will reward.

2. **Quantified low-stakes win as a credibility deposit.** "During the second quarter of 2025, only five errors were reported with no adverse effects on residents. Staff received education, and the Consultant Pharmacist, Pharmacy, Physicians, and interdisciplinary team investigated all errors and provided recommendations for improvement." "Only five" plus "no adverse effects" is a concrete, bounded data point; the named roster shows the closed detect-investigate-recommend loop. This is the evidentiary basis for the substantially-compliant findings, supplied before any deficiency is raised.

3. **Deficiency pivot with continuity verb.** "[Facility] nursing staff continues to struggle with consistently administering residents' medications and treatments within the correct timeframe, particularly during the 9:00 a.m. medication pass." "Continues to struggle" mirrors the opening's "continues to educate" — the same continuity verb now carries the deficiency, signaling a known, tracked, longitudinal problem. The narrowing clause scopes the defect to a fixable locus and sets up the remediation story.

4. **Remediation-before-data (facility shown working the problem).** "Recognizing this issue, the clinical team, including the medical director, reviewed the most common medications... The team eliminated sliding scale insulin administration, adjusted vital sign parameters, and modified medication times when possible (e.g., changing from 9:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m.)." Immediately after naming the deficiency, three concrete interventions with a worked example. The reader sees engaged, root-cause problem-solving, not just a problem. "Recognizing this issue" attributes self-awareness to the facility.

5. **Shift-by-shift compliance data presented as prose.** "Data collected over the second quarter of 2025 showed 73% compliance on the 7-3 shift, 79% on the 3-11 shift, and 100% on the 11-7 shift. Overall quarterly compliance was 84%..." The three figures inline let the reader instantly locate the problem (day shift, lowest at 73%) and corroborate the earlier "particularly during the 9:00 a.m. medication pass" claim. Disaggregation tells a truer story than a single average would — granular enough to be diagnostic, written as sentences so it stays inside the narrative.

6. **Inline baseline weaving (then-vs-now in one clause).** "Overall quarterly compliance was 84%, an improvement from the first quarter's 76% compliance." The baseline is woven into the same sentence as the current figure and explicitly labeled "an improvement" — no separate paragraph, no table. The comparison is unavoidable and the trajectory is stated as the writer's own conclusion, which lets the section credit progress while the headline still reads "not reached substantial compliance."

7. **Root-cause analysis with self-imposed threshold.** The RCA causes ("large quantities... particularly on the day shift") tie back to and explain the 73% figure, closing the analytic loop. "continued auditing until reaching at least 80% compliance" shows the facility set its own measurable exit criterion — exactly the QAPI behavior the Decree rewards. The specific improvement actions let the reader later judge follow-through rather than taking "we made a plan" on faith.

8. **System narrative — implementation and optimism.** "In August 2025, [Facility] implemented the Passport Medication Administration System (Passport). The monitoring team interviewed several nurses who expressed confidence that the system will increase efficiency once nurses become more comfortable with it." A dated milestone and a parenthetical short-name convention for clean downstream reference. Sourcing the optimism to nurse interviews keeps the monitor neutral. The hedge "once nurses become more comfortable" plants the expectation that the system is not yet delivering.

9. **The "However" reversal anchored to the onsite visit.** "However, during the October 2025 onsite visit, the monitoring team observed that on two units, three of four nurses did not complete the 9:00 a.m. medication pass on time. One nurse stated that when a resident's scheduled medication is not in the dispensed envelope, she must go to the Passport dispensing machine to retrieve it..." The pivot turns staff optimism into first-hand observed shortfall, precise ("two units, three of four nurses") not impressionistic. The nurse's mechanistic explanation surfaces the system-design root cause the committee paragraph will later diagnose. The new system is credited as adopted but honestly shown not yet curing the underlying problem.

10. **Documentation deficiency with epistemic honesty.** "Another TAR lacked initials for all treatments scheduled on a weekday 7-3 shift. The monitoring team could not determine whether treatments were completed but not documented, or not completed at all." The monitor declines to overclaim, isolating the actual finding (a documentation failure) from a graver one it cannot substantiate (a care failure). This restraint grounds the 50c/50d Partially Compliant placement.

11. **Observed-practice deficiency tied to standard of practice.** "During two separate onsite visits, the monitoring team observed two nurses paging through medication administration binders and making entries on various pages after completing medication administration. Standard of practice requires documenting medications at the time of administration." Two independent observations establish a pattern, not a one-off; the closing sentence supplies the normative benchmark the behavior violated. The explicit "standard of practice requires..." construction converts an observation into a defensible deficiency finding.

12. **Committee narrative diagnosing the system's structural limit.** "The discussion revealed that Passport capacity concerns are the biggest issue. Specifically, physicians order large quantities of medications with varying doses for each resident. Each dose requires its own Passport slot, limiting capacity for additional medications. The committee also identified that census growth may result in insufficient machine capacity." Elevates the single nurse's anecdote into a structural diagnosis reached through the facility's own governance body — demonstrating a functioning improvement process (credit) even as it confirms the timeliness problem is not yet solved (deficiency). The clearest illustration of separating "the system for fixing problems works" from "the problem is fixed."

13. **Closing on substantially-compliant evidence before the verdict.** "[Facility] nursing staff record the effectiveness of as-needed medication on the reverse side of the medication administration record... The pharmacy consultant routinely reviews residents' medication regimens... Physicians review these documented consults in residents' medical records." The section ends not on a deficiency but on functioning practices that map to the substantially-compliant provisions, cushioning the "has not reached substantial compliance" headline and visually justifying why six of eight provisions land in the Substantially Compliant row.

14. **Determination headline that names the verdict but the table that distributes it.** The prose verdict is uniformly negative, but the table tells the nuanced truth: only two provisions (50c timeliness, 50d documentation) are Partially Compliant, six are Substantially Compliant, none Non-Compliant. A single failing provision holds the whole Remedial Measure short of substantial compliance while the table preserves credit for everything that works. The empty Non-Compliant row is itself a finding: nothing is broken, two things are merely incomplete. This mapping is what lets one section be honestly both "improving" and "not yet compliant."

## How to Use These

Before drafting any care area, identify which exemplar your source material most resembles in shape, then study that one closely:

- **Spare or deficiency-heavy section** (the facility is failing, or there is little quantitative data) → lean on **Medication Administration**'s deficiency-with-progress moves: credit-first opening, continuity-verb pivot, observation tied to standard of practice, epistemic honesty about what the record can prove, and a determination table that parks only the failing provisions in Partially Compliant.
- **Data-dense section** (PBJ metrics, audited shift counts, anything genuinely tabular) → model **Staffing**: every figure paired with its benchmark, native units for each source, sparing nested bullets only where prose would bury the pattern, italic run-in sub-headings to segment, and interpretation immediately after presentation.
- **Positive / reached-compliance section** → model **Oversight and Management of Medical Care**: commendation anchored to credentials and counted conduct, the monitor's impression leashed to observable behavior, residual concerns surfaced with their corrective arc, and a table that reconciles the positive headline with any lingering partial.

Match the chosen exemplar's register and structure, then fill it with **your source file's facts** — the real dates, counts, agendas, shift figures, role observations, and Consent Decree paragraph numbers. Do not import the exemplars' specific numbers or names; they are scaffolding, not content. Keep the voice flat and declarative, the evidence specific and sourced, the baseline inline and sparing, and always close with the Compliance Determination paragraph and the full three-row table. Use `[Facility]`, no case number, no resident or staff names, and no observation IDs in the prose.
